# Will the Customer Accept the Coupon?

## Project Summary

This project looks at the kinds of drivers who accepted mobile coupons while traveling. The analysis uses 12,684 driving scenarios and compares people who accepted a coupon with people who declined it.

## Main Findings

- Overall, **56.84%** of the coupons were accepted and **43.16%** were declined.
- Bar coupons had a lower acceptance rate than the overall average: **41.00%**.
- Previous interest in going to bars was strongly related to accepting a bar coupon:
  - Drivers who went to a bar three or fewer times per month accepted **37.06%** of the bar coupons.
  - Drivers who went more than three times accepted **76.88%**.
- Drivers who went to a bar more than once a month and were over 25 accepted **69.52%** of the coupons, compared with **33.50%** for everyone else.
- The strongest bar-coupon group tested was made up of drivers who went to bars more than once a month, did not have a child passenger, and worked outside farming, fishing, or forestry. This group accepted **71.32%** of its coupons, compared with **29.60%** for everyone else.
- For Coffee House coupons, acceptance also rose with prior interest in coffee houses. Acceptance was **18.88%** among drivers who never visited a coffee house, compared with about **65% to 69%** among drivers who visited one or more times regularly.

## Conclusion

Customers were more likely to accept a coupon when it matched an activity they already enjoyed. Bar coupons were especially popular with frequent bar visitors, older drivers, and drivers without a child passenger. Coffee House coupons showed a similar pattern: people who already visited coffee houses were more receptive.

These findings describe patterns in the survey data. They show relationships between customer characteristics and coupon acceptance, but they do not prove that any one characteristic directly caused a customer to accept a coupon.

## Data Preparation

The `car` column was removed because almost all of its values were missing. The remaining missing category values were labeled as `Unknown`, allowing the analysis to keep all observations.
